There's a quarterly review I still think about.

Sales blamed marketing for poor leads.

Marketing blamed the data.

And customer success sat there with three churned accounts nobody saw coming.

Everyone had a slide, everyone had a reason, and everyone was, in their own way, right.

Nobody in that room was failing.

The system between them was.

That's the moment it clicked for me.

I had moved from door-to-door sales into HR, marketing, sales enablement, CRM and finally RevOps, and the problem was always the same one wearing a different outfit.

People, process and technology not talking to each other.

And the communication problem doesn't stay with the humans. It leaks into the systems they design too. How deep-rooted!

## What it looks like from the inside

If you're a founder, you probably know the signs.

The quarterly review turns into a blame session.

You bought the tool, and six months later the team is back in Excel.

You're screaming, "I didn't spend a few hundred thousand dollars to switch back to Excel."

The business works, but only when you're in the room.

So you do what most founders do.

You hire.

A new head of sales, a marketing lead, maybe someone for customer success.

And for a quarter or two, things look better.

Then the same arguments come back with new faces.

That's because GTM problems are rarely hiring problems.

If the handoff between marketing and sales is broken, a better salesperson just inherits a broken handoff.

## Alignment is a meeting

The usual fix is "alignment".

A weekly sync, a shared dashboard, an offsite with sticky notes.

I'm not against any of it.

But alignment is a meeting.

It lasts until everyone goes back to their desk.

Integration is a system.

It's agreeing, in writing and in your CRM, what a qualified lead actually is.

Who picks it up, and by when.

What sales hands to customer success when a deal closes.

Which number everyone uses for the forecast, so there's one version of the truth instead of three.

None of that needs a new team.

It needs someone to own the space between the teams.

## Where to start

Pick the one handoff that hurts most, usually marketing to sales or sales to customer success, and walk it end to end.

Literally follow one lead, or one new customer, through every step.

Write down every place it waits, gets re-entered, or gets dropped.

Then fix that one gap properly before you buy anything or hire anyone.

Agree the definition, the owner and the timing, and put it in the system so it happens without someone chasing.

You'll be surprised how much "performance" improves when people stop working around the gaps.

## In conclusion

Most businesses don't have a sales problem or a marketing problem.

They have an integration problem.

And the good news is that integration problems are fixable, usually without a single new hire.

The next time your quarterly review turns into a blame session, don't ask who dropped the ball.

Ask where the ball had nowhere to go.
