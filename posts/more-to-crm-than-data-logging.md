I recently met a founder who was making a decision on which CRM to invest in. Or so I thought.

He runs an SME in infrastructure, a few million dollars in turnover, doing well regionally and in huge expansion mode.

We got talking, and much like every conversation I have, it landed on CRM.

His view: AI will do everything, so why would a company like his need a CRM at all?

For him, a CRM was a data log that spits out reports.

We debated for a bit, until I knew I wasn't winning this one. So I changed the topic.

I was careful not to get defensive.

I'm passionate about this, so it takes effort (just ask the man of the house).

When a founder walks in with a strong opinion, he's usually done his research and found people who agree with him.

One coin, two sides, and he only wants to see one.

I'd much rather show him with a proof of concept than win an argument.

An offended prospect won't become a client.

So here's what I didn't say in that room.

## What is a CRM, really?

Most people think it's where salespeople log contacts and deals. That's the software. The idea is bigger.

A CRM is your company's memory: every conversation, promise and contract, long after the person who made it has left.

It's your rulebook: how a lead becomes a deal and who does what at each handoff.

And it's your compass: what's in the pipeline, what's at risk, where to put your money next.

Data logging is the least interesting part.

And yes, AI is about to take it over. Fair enough!

## Why should you even use one?

Because without it, your company's memory walks out of the door every time someone resigns.

I was implementing a CRM for a client recently who wanted everything captured: field visits, call transcripts, every detail of every interaction.

Endearing at first, then a tad much. So I asked him why.

He told me he'd once lost a large multi-year contract because the account manager had promised the client things at renewal the team couldn't deliver.

Nobody else knew.

The deal was in the forecast, and then it was gone. Real pain!

That's the cost of a relationship living in one person's head.

## What can AI do, and what can't it?

He did have a point.

AI can capture calls and emails, summarise an account, draft the follow-up and spot the deal that's gone quiet.

What it can't do on its own is be the one record everyone trusts, decide who sees which customer, or enforce how a deal moves.

Salesforce's own researchers [tested AI agents on CRM tasks](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/16/salesforce_llm_agents_benchmark/): they managed about 58% of simple tasks, and only 35% of tasks with several steps.

So use AI, by all means.

Just don't hand it your customer relationships with nothing underneath.

## Can a self-built tool on an LLM really replace a CRM?

You can build one.

But you'll need a data model, permissions, an audit trail, integrations, backups and data rules for every country you expand into.

By then, guess what, you've built a CRM.

Except now you own every bug, and one person who understands it.

## Does it give anything other than reports with questionable data?

Honestly? Often it doesn't. CRM is garbage in, garbage out.

But bad data is a process problem, not a software one.

It goes bad when nobody owns it, when there are sixty fields nobody understands, and when people update the CRM for the Monday report instead of for their own work.

In Salesforce's [2026 State of Sales survey](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-sales-report-announcement-2026/), 79% of high performers prioritise data hygiene, against 54% of underperformers.

Salesforce sells CRM, so take it with a pinch of salt.

Still hard to argue with.

## CRM in infrastructure

Industries like his don't need a CRM less. They need it differently.

Construction ranks second to last on [McKinsey's digitisation index](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/operations/our-insights/reinventing-construction-through-a-productivity-revolution) in the US, and last in Europe.

Yet deals take a year or more, tenders involve owners, consultants, contractors and financiers, and the reasons you won or lost a bid usually sit in a folder someone made once.

Add international expansion, with new partners and new rules, and the case only gets stronger.

When one relationship is worth millions, losing it because it lived in someone's phone is the most expensive data problem there is.

## What is the future of it all?

Here's where I think it goes.

CRMs will read every conversation, across chat, calls, email and meetings, and pull it into one place.

They'll add the analysis and key takeaways for each deal, work out the chance of closing from your history, look at the client's needs, your capabilities and the competition, and suggest two or three solutions, each with a likelihood of winning.

Once the deal is won, they'll adjust the forecast, commissions and revenue numbers on their own.

Everything you'd need an army to do, done for you, so all you have to do is read it and make better decisions.

That future still runs on a CRM.

It just stops being a data log.

## Is it worth the hype, or just another overhead?

It's an overhead if you buy it to log data.

It's an asset if you use it to run the business.

Start with the decisions you need to make, then the few fields that feed them, then the tool.

## In conclusion

I didn't win that debate in the room, and on one point he was right. Nobody should be logging data by hand anymore.

But if you feel strongly that AI can replace your CRM, test it.

Ask for a proof of concept.

Either it proves you right, or it changes your mind. Both are worth more than an argument.

=== FAQ
Does AI replace CRM?|No. AI can capture, summarise and draft, but it needs a CRM underneath to act as the trusted record, control who sees what, and enforce how deals move. AI makes a CRM easier to use, not unnecessary.
Can I build my own CRM on ChatGPT or another LLM?|You can, but you'd have to build and maintain the data model, permissions, audit trail, integrations and compliance yourself. For most SMEs, that's a custom CRM with one person who understands it.
Do infrastructure and construction companies need a CRM?|Yes, often more than other businesses. Long sales cycles, tenders, many decision makers, consortium partners and expansion into new markets all depend on remembering relationships over years.
Why is CRM data so often wrong?|Usually because of process, not software: too many fields, no clear owner, and people updating it for the report rather than for their work. Fewer fields, clear ownership and AI-assisted capture fix most of it.
What should a founder do first?|Start with the decisions you need to make, then the few data points that inform them, then pick the tool.
