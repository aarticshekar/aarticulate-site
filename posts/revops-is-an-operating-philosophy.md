Every few months someone tells me they've "set up RevOps".

Usually it means they've hired one person, given them the CRM admin login, and put them somewhere between sales and finance on the org chart.

Two quarters later that person is drowning in report requests, and nothing about how the teams work together has changed.

I get why it happens. A department is easy to create. You can draw a box, give it a budget, and feel like you've done something.

But RevOps works as a way of running the business, not as a box.

## What RevOps actually does

At its simplest, RevOps makes sure sales, marketing and customer success run off the same plan, the same data and the same rules.

One definition of a lead. One pipeline. One forecast. One view of the customer from first click to renewal.

That sounds obvious, until you try it.

Marketing measures leads. Sales measures closed deals. Customer success measures renewals.

Each team is doing well by its own number, and the business still leaks revenue in the handoffs between them.

RevOps is the job of owning those handoffs.

## Why a department isn't enough

When RevOps is just a team, the other teams treat it as a service desk.

Need a report? Ask RevOps. Field missing? Ask RevOps. Forecast wrong? Must be the data, ask RevOps.

The real work, getting leaders to agree on definitions, rules and who owns what, never happens, because nobody gave RevOps the authority to make it happen.

When RevOps is an operating philosophy, the leadership team owns it together.

They agree how revenue flows through the business, and RevOps builds and protects the system that makes it happen.

The RevOps person is the one keeping everyone honest to what was agreed, not the report-builder.

## What that looks like in practice

Building commercial functions from scratch taught me that the structure matters less than the agreements.

Who owns a lead at each stage. What "qualified" means. What sales must hand over when a deal closes. How often the forecast is reviewed and by whom.

Get those written down, built into the CRM and reviewed regularly, and you have RevOps, whatever the job titles say.

Skip them, and you have a very busy CRM admin.

## In conclusion

If you're thinking about RevOps, don't start with a hire.

Start with a conversation between your sales, marketing and customer success leaders about how revenue actually moves through your business, and where it gets stuck.

The hire comes after. And when it does, give them the mandate to hold everyone to what you agreed, not just a login.
