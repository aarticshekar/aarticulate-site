Ask marketing why sales isn't converting their leads and you'll hear that sales doesn't follow up.

Ask sales and you'll hear the leads are rubbish.

Both are usually telling the truth, and both are missing the point.

The problem is what happens when the score is reached.

## How it usually goes

A lead downloads something, opens a few emails, visits the pricing page.

The scoring model ticks past a number and the lead becomes an MQL. It lands in a sales queue with a name, an email and a score.

The salesperson has no idea why this person is interested, what they read, or what problem they might have.

They call, get voicemail, move on. A week later the lead is cold, and marketing's dashboard still counts it as a success.

That's a handoff with nothing in it, not a lead quality problem.

## What a good handoff looks like

When I've seen MQL to SQL conversion move properly (in one case, by 40%), it was never because someone found a cleverer scoring model.

It was because marketing and sales agreed a few simple things and stuck to them.

What counts as a lead worth a salesperson's time, in plain words, not just points.

What travels with it: what they did, what they read, what they seem to care about.

Who picks it up, and how fast. And what happens if sales decides it's not ready, so it goes back to marketing instead of into a black hole.

Then you put all of that into the CRM, so it happens every time without anyone chasing.

## The feedback loop nobody builds

The most valuable part is the bit most teams skip.

When sales rejects a lead, they say why.

When a lead turns into a deal, marketing hears about it.

Over a few months that feedback tells you far more about lead quality than any scoring model.

Without it, marketing optimises for MQL volume and sales quietly stops trusting the queue.

With it, both teams start working off the same definition of a good lead.

## In conclusion

If your MQL to SQL conversion is stuck, resist the urge to rebuild the scoring model.

Sit marketing and sales in the same room and agree what gets handed over, by whom, how fast, and what happens when it bounces back.

The score tells you when to pass the ball. The handoff decides whether anyone catches it.
